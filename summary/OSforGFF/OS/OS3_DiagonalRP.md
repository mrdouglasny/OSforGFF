via a *diagonal kernel* argument, avoiding any spatial Fourier decomposition of the test
function. The strategy has four stages: (1) compute $\lVert \Theta x - x \rVert = 2\lvert x_0 \rvert$,
showing the distance depends only on the time component; (2) use the Schwinger representation

positive-time $x$; and (4) show that any pair $(x, y)$ can be normalized to axis form
$(t, \vec{0}), (-t, \vec{0})$ by a Euclidean motion.
