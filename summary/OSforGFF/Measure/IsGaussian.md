## Overview

Proves that the free Gaussian Free Field measure constructed via Minlos is Gaussian in the
Glimm–Jaffe sense (`isGaussianGJ`), and that its complex two-point Schwinger function equals the
free covariance. Everything is dimension-generic: the section variables are
`{d : ℕ} [Fact (2 ≤ d)] (m : ℝ) [Fact (0 < m)] [GFFPropagator d m]`, and the root-level theorems
carry the same hypotheses explicitly.

The identification $S_2^{\mathbb{C}}(f, g) = C_\mathbb{C}(f, g)$ is obtained in two stages. On
*real* test functions the polarization identity reduces $\int \langle\omega,f\rangle
\langle\omega,g\rangle\, d\mu$ to the real covariance form $C_m$ (`schwinger_eq_covariance_real`);
this is then lifted to complex arguments by bilinearity. In parallel, the complex generating
functional $Z[J] = \exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac12 C_\mathbb{C}(J, J)\bigr)$ is established from the real
characteristic functional by extending in two complex parameters: OS0 gives analyticity of
$Z[z_0 f + z_1 g]$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$, and the 1-D identity theorem (applied twice, at
accumulation points of $\mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}$) matches it against the entire Gaussian
formula — so the proof needs no `twoD_line_from_realCF` from `MinlosAnalytic`. Combining centering
with the Gaussian generating functional yields `isGaussianGJ_gaussianFreeField_free`.

Notation: $Z[\cdot]$ is `GJGeneratingFunctionalℂ`, $S_2^{\mathbb{C}}$ is `SchwingerFunctionℂ₂`,
$C_m$ is `freeCovarianceFormR m` (real form), and $C_\mathbb{C}$ is `freeCovarianceℂ_bilinear m`.
